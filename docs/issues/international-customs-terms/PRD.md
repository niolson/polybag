# International customs terms: duties, seller tax registrations and required IDs

Status: reference

Repo: `polybag`

Background for the issues in this directory. Not a work item. The decisions are recorded
in [ADR-0008](../../adr/0008-customs-terms-resolution.md).

> *This was generated by AI during triage.*

## Where this came from

A design session on 2026-10-01 that started as "should PolyBag support DDP?" and became a
review of everything an international label declares. It was grilled the same day into
the decisions below. The product-identifier half of the EU change had already shipped as
[`eu-product-identifiers`](../eu-product-identifiers/PRD.md); this is the rest.

No tenant ships to EU consumers yet, and there are no live tenants, so nothing here has
to preserve current behavior.

## The problem

Every international label PolyBag buys today declares fixed terms, whoever the client is
and whatever the order says:

| Where | What it does today | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| `FedexAdapter::buildRateApiRequest()` and `::buildCustomsClearanceDetail()` | `dutiesPayment.paymentType = SENDER` on every international rate and label; no `termsOfSale`; no IOSS | Duties and VAT billed to the carrier account weeks later, for every destination; EU VAT paid twice when the order already collected it |
| `UpsAdapter::createShipment()` | Only `ShipmentCharge` Type `01` (transportation) | DDU everywhere |
| `UspsAdapter` label request | Never sends `prepayDutiesTaxesFees` | DDU everywhere, including the six EU countries where USPS *requires* DDP |
| `UspsAdapter::buildCustomsForm()` | `AESITN` hard-coded to `NO EEI 30.37(a)`; UPS and FedEx send no export filing at all | A false exemption claim on any parcel over $2,500 |
| `UspsAdapter::buildCustomsForm()` | `countryofOrigin` falls back to `US` | A false declaration for imported goods |
| All adapters | No seller tax registration, no recipient tax ID | VAT charged again at the border; Brazil and South Korea parcels held |

Since 1 July 2026 every EU import pays a €3 duty per tariff line, and a handling fee of
about €2 follows by 1 November. DDU parcels collect those plus the carrier's or postal
operator's own fee (€7.50 at Deutsche Post; €15–25 reported elsewhere) at the door, and
the postal networks are starting to refuse them. DDP is becoming the normal way to sell
into the EU.

## Three separate questions

Each international shipment answers three questions, and they combine freely.

| Question | Values | Who decides |
|---|---|---|
| Who pays duties and import charges? | DDP (the carrier account) or DDU (the recipient) | The order, else the client's policy |
| Was import VAT already collected at checkout? | A seller tax registration for the destination, or none | The order's registration, else the client's |
| Which IDs does the destination need? | Recipient tax ID, export ITN | Destination rules plus order data |

**IOSS and DDP are complementary.** IOSS proves the VAT was collected, so customs does not
charge it again; DDP pays whatever else is due, including the €3 duty, which IOSS does
not cover. The usual EU combination is IOSS plus DDP. DDP without the IOSS number means
the VAT is paid twice.

A 3PL sees every combination at once: one client on Shopify Markets collecting duties at
checkout with its own IOSS, one with no EU registration at all, one selling on a
marketplace that collected the VAT under the marketplace's own IOSS.

## Decisions

Settled in the grilling session; the reasoning and the rejected options are in ADR-0008.

1. **A client's duties policy starts unset, and an EU label is refused until someone
   chooses.** Neither default is safe: DDU walks parcels into postal refusals and door
   fees, DDP bills duties to whichever carrier account the scope resolves, possibly the
   3PL's own. Destinations outside the EU are DDU until set otherwise.
2. **The policy is a map from destination to term.** A destination is `EU` or an ISO
   country code; the most specific entry wins; values are `ddp` or `ddu`, nothing else.
3. **Carrier duties support is sourced data, not code.**
   `resources/data/customs/duties-support.json` gives each carrier and destination one of
   `ddp_required`, `either` or `ddu_only`, with a source and a checked date per entry,
   optional effective dates, and a version stamp. A rate whose support does not fit the
   resolved term is **dropped, with the reason named** — never forced to DDP, never
   silently sent DDU.
4. **The order's seller tax registration replaces the client's**, never merges with it.
   A marketplace that is the deemed supplier collected the VAT under its own number, and
   the client's number would declare the wrong seller.
5. **The order's duties term overrides the client's policy in either direction**, and
   counts as the choice decision 1 requires.
6. **There is no packer-level override.** The Shipment's `duties_terms` is the override,
   edited by a manager (`ShipmentPolicy::update()` already requires `Role::Manager`), and
   the resolved terms are part of the Offer fingerprint.
7. **A client has one tax registration per regime**, and the regime fixes its
   destinations, its number format and its low-value threshold.
8. **Amazon Buy Shipping and Shopify Shipping offers have source-decided terms.** A person
   may pick them; automation does not buy them for EU, GB, NO or AU destinations until a
   test purchase shows what the source does.
9. **USPS DDP terms acceptance belongs to the USPS `CarrierAccount`**, recorded by an Admin,
   and gates USPS DDP rates.
10. **The recipient tax ID is two typed columns on the Shipment**, required where a sourced
    data file says, and covered by PII retention.
11. **Any customs total over $2,500 needs an ITN**, except to Canada. Grouping by six-digit
    HS code, which is what the regulation actually measures, is deferred.
12. **A missing country of origin blocks every international label; a missing or short HS
    code blocks EU and UK labels.** The USPS `US` fallback goes. No grace period.
13. **One `CustomsReadiness` checker** is authoritative at purchase and previewed on the
    Ship page. The zero-value and product-identifier guards move into it.
14. **The Label records what was declared**: a `customs_terms` snapshot and, for USPS DDP,
    the `duties_cost` USPS returns. No duty quotes at rate time yet.
15. **Rate requests carry the resolved terms**, so a DDP surcharge is in the Offer's price.

### Tax registration regimes

| Regime | Destinations | Format | Low-value threshold | Measured on |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `ioss` | EU-27; also Northern Ireland, at £135 | `IM` + 10 digits | €150 | The whole consignment: intrinsic value, goods only |
| `uk_vat` | GB, including Northern Ireland | `GB` + 9 or 12 digits | £135 | The whole consignment: intrinsic value, goods only |
| `voec` | NO | 7 digits | under NOK 3,000 (2,999 or less) | **Each item**, shipping excluded |
| `arn` | AU | 12 digits | AUD 1,000 | **Each item**, customs value |

*Intrinsic value* is the price of the goods, without transport and insurance shown
separately on the invoice, and without other taxes and charges.

**Sending a registration over its threshold is not harmless.** For IOSS, customs ignores
the number and charges VAT on the whole consignment, and the seller must refund the VAT
it collected at sale (Commission explanatory notes, Q24). Over the threshold, the
registration is not sent and the checker warns.

The per-item regimes need a rule of their own:

- **VOEC:** items under NOK 3,000 may share a parcel even when the total is over it, but
  an item at NOK 3,000 or more "cannot" ship in the same parcel (Skatteetaten). The
  checker blocks: split that item into its own Shipment.
- **ARN:** each item at AUD 1,000 or less keeps its GST-paid status inside a consignment
  that totals more. Australian customs marks each item as paid or not (Home Affairs
  Notice 2018/14, scenario 2). Whether UPS, FedEx or USPS can carry that per-item mark is
  `02`'s question. Until it is answered, the checker blocks a parcel to AU that holds both
  an item over AUD 1,000 and a registration to declare.

**Northern Ireland** may use an EU IOSS registration instead of UK VAT, for consignments
of £135 or less of goods located outside GB at sale (HMRC). When a client has both, IOSS
is sent for NI and UK VAT for the rest of GB.

**Exchange rate.** Customs values are in USD. The EU tests IOSS eligibility at the ECB
rate on the day payment was accepted. Norway accepts any central bank's rate, used
consistently. The UK and Australian rules are unconfirmed. The ECB publishes daily
reference rates for USD, GBP, NOK and AUD, so one cached daily ECB rate covers all four.
The rate is taken on the order date, or the Shipment's import date when the order date
is not known.

**Changes ahead.** The EU's removal of the €150 IOSS cap is at the earliest 1 July 2028,
and sources disagree on whether it has been adopted. The UK ends its low-value duty
relief by October 2028 at the latest and has not decided how VAT will be collected after
that. Neither changes anything now. The thresholds live with the regime, so either change
is one edit.

Research of 2026-10-07; quotes and links are in *Sources*. Some quotes came from page
summaries, so check the source before citing it.

### Recipient tax ID types

`cpf`, `cnpj` (Brazil; check digits), `pccc` (South Korea; `P` + 12 digits), `vat`,
`other`. Where each is required lives in `resources/data/customs/recipient-tax-id.json`.

## Resolution

```
duties term   = shipment.duties_terms
              ?? client.duties_policy[country]
              ?? client.duties_policy["EU"]   (EU destinations)
              ?? DDU                           (non-EU destinations)
              — an EU destination with none of the first three is unresolved: refused

registration  = shipment's registration, when its regime covers the destination
              ?? the client's registration for the regime that covers the destination
              — omitted when the consignment (IOSS, UK VAT) is over that regime's
                threshold; per item for VOEC and ARN, see *Tax registration regimes*
```

Rate filtering then applies `duties-support.json` to the resolved term. Offers from a
source that cannot take terms skip the filter and are recorded as `source_decided`.

## Carrier field mapping

Checked against the sandboxes on 2026-10-08 (`02`, which has the evidence). A *qualified*
cell is the best field available, with the doubt named in `02`.

| Value | USPS (International Labels 3.3.11) | UPS (Shipping API) | FedEx (Ship API) |
|---|---|---|---|
| DDP | `packageDescription.prepayDutiesTaxesFees: true`; cost returned in `prepaidDutiesTaxesFees`; `030031` where unavailable | Second `ShipmentCharge`, Type `02`, `BillShipper`, **and** `TermsOfShipment: DDP` | `dutiesPayment.paymentType: SENDER` + payor account, and `termsOfSale: DDP` |
| DDP surcharge in a rate | Not quoted; landed cost comes back on the label | Rating with the Type `02` charge returns itemized charge `378` | Not quoted: Rate API takes only `SENDER`; duty estimates (`edtRequestType`) are barred for FedEx Compatible solutions |
| DDU | Omit the flag | Only Type `01` | `paymentType: RECIPIENT`, `termsOfSale: DDU` |
| Terms on the invoice | — | `InternationalForms.TermsOfShipment` | `commercialInvoice.termsOfSale` |
| IOSS / VOEC / ARN | `customsForm.exportersReference`, `VAT_NUMBER` (with prepay it suppresses the landed cost: *qualified*) | `ShipFrom.VendorInfo`, `0356` / `0357` / `1052` | Shipper `tins`; no IOSS type, `BUSINESS_UNION` prints as EORI (*qualified*) |
| UK VAT | `exportersReference` (*qualified*) | No current code: `0358` deprecated and dropped, `0000` unlabelled (*qualified*) | Shipper `tins`, `BUSINESS_NATIONAL` (*qualified*) |
| Recipient tax ID | `customsForm.importersReference` (`TAX_CODE`) | `GlobalTaxInformation.AgentTaxIdentificationNumber`, role `30`, type `0005` / `1002` | Recipient `tins`, `PERSONAL_NATIONAL` (prints); `recipientCustomsId` does not |
| Export ITN or exemption | `customsForm.AESITN` | EEI form (`FormType` `11`), `EEIFilingOption` `1`, `ShipperFiled` `A` + ITN or `B` + legend | `exportDetail.exportComplianceStatement`: `AESX…` or `NO_EEI_30_37_A`; FedEx prints 30.37(a) itself if omitted |
| Per-item tax paid (AU) | None | `Product.TaxesPaid`, Singapore only | None |

### USPS DDP coverage, from the International Mail Manual

Applies to Priority Mail Express International, Priority Mail International and
First-Class Package International.

| Support | Countries |
|---|---|
| **Required** for dutiable items | DE, BE, DK, FI, FR, PT, LU, MC (gifts up to €45 excepted) |
| Optional | AT, EE, IT, MT, NL, SK, ES, SE; also CA, GB |
| Not available | BG, HR, CY, CZ, GR, HU, IE, LV, LT, PL, RO, SI |

UPS and FedEx express accept DDU throughout the EU as of 2026-10-01; the refusals are a
postal-network problem. UPS Worldwide Economy DDU to Germany is reported suspended.

## Validation

Run by `CustomsReadiness` (`05`), at purchase and as a Ship-page preview.

| Rule | Applies to | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Duties term resolved | EU | Block |
| Every line has a country of origin | All international | Block |
| Every line has an HS code of 6+ digits | EU, GB | Block |
| HS code present | Other international | Warn |
| Customs total over $2,500 has an ITN | All international except CA, and the always-file destinations | Block |
| Recipient tax ID present | Per `recipient-tax-id.json` (BR, KR to start) | Block |
| Line value is not zero | All international | Block (existing guard, moved) |
| EU consumer line has M-PID and NS-PID | EU consumer | Block (existing guard, moved) |
| DDP with no seller registration for a regime that has one | EU, GB, NO, AU | Warn: VAT may be charged twice |
| Consignment over the IOSS or UK VAT threshold | EU, GB | Warn: registration not sent |
| An item at NOK 3,000 or more in a parcel declared under VOEC | NO | Block: ship that item separately |
| An item over AUD 1,000 in a parcel with an ARN to declare | AU | Block until `02` finds a per-item GST-paid mark |
| USPS description cut at 30 characters | USPS | Warn |

Rate-dependent rules stay in the rate filter: a USPS DDP-required country for a DDU
order, a USPS DDP-not-available country for a DDP order, a USPS account that has not
accepted the DDP terms.

## Non-goals

- Filing EEI. PolyBag records the ITN; the operator files in AESDirect.
- Licensed goods. PolyBag has no ECCN data and does not check for export licenses.
- Anything a Shopify Shipping or Amazon Buy Shipping label declares — their terms are
  source-decided until verified (`09`).

## Open questions

All but the last two are `02`'s. On 2026-10-08 `02` answered the USPS IOSS field, prepay
to an optional country (it works), the UPS DDP surcharge in rates, and where each
carrier takes the ITN; the spec text says the USPS DDP terms are accepted by sending
the flag. Still open:

- The FedEx `tinType` customs reads as IOSS; what USPS does with IOSS + prepay; a UK
  VAT code on UPS. Who to ask is in `02`.
- Does USPS production refuse a DDP-required country without the flag? TEM does not.
- What happens after 31 October to a USPS DDP parcel into Germany that carries no IOSS
  number? Deutsche Post's transition period for non-IOSS postal parcels is reported to
  end then; no German official source confirms the date.
  Research on 2026-10-07 found no Deutsche Post or DHL source either. Swiss Post says
  prepaid duties become mandatory from 1 November 2026 for all business-to-consumer
  parcels up to €150 into Germany. Until USPS or Deutsche Post confirms otherwise,
  assume a USPS parcel into Germany without both DDP and an IOSS number is at risk.
- The UK: which exchange rate tests the £135 limit, and where the seller's UK VAT number
  goes in the UK customs data. Australia: the exchange-rate source, and whether customs
  value excludes freight. None of these is confirmed by an official source.
- Which Shopify order field reliably says duties were charged at checkout (`10`).
- What Amazon Buy Shipping and Shopify Shipping declare for duties terms (`09`).
