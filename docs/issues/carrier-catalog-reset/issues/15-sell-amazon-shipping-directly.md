# Sell Amazon Shipping directly for orders from other channels

Status: needs-triage

Repo: `polybag`

> *This was generated by AI during triage.*

## Parent

[PRD](../PRD.md), *Catalog* and *Which sources can sell a service*. ADR-0006 decisions 1,
3 and 6, and option M.

## What to build

Amazon Shipping sold to a Shopify, Database or manual order is a direct sale, bought the
way UPS is. It sells one carrier through one account, priced and known before purchase.
Shipping v2 is the only API Amazon Shipping has. A method lists Amazon Shipping Ground,
the scoped connection is its account, and automation buys it like any listed direct
service. Buy Shipping keeps Amazon's own orders.

- **The carrier.** Amazon Shipping (seeded in `11`) gets `adapter = amazon_shipping`. A
  direct adapter is registered under that key.
- **The account is the scoped connection.** Nothing about routing changes except the
  carrier row (ADR-0002's 2026-09-22 amendment): four precedence bands, `rate_shop`
  always off, and a client's own connection scoped only to that client.
  - The scope moves from the `Amazon` row to Amazon Shipping, and
    `CarrierAccountScope::amazonCarrierId()` becomes Amazon Shipping's. The migration moves
    existing scopes rather than letting the `Amazon` row's cascade delete them.
  - Opting a connection into `offers_off_amazon_shipping` still creates the default scope
    when none exists, now on Amazon Shipping.
  - The adapter declares that its account is a connection (`05`). The Carrier Account form
    lists Amazon Shipping with a link to Connections rather than a form, and
    `CarrierAccount` refuses to save against it.
- **Quoting.** The adapter sends `channelType: EXTERNAL` on the scoped connection. It
  reuses the request `amazon-shipping-external-orders/05` built: items from what was
  packed, their weights scaled to fit, and `403 A-101` reported as the connection not
  being set up. The Buy Shipping adapter stops quoting `EXTERNAL`.
  - It reads its services from the catalog by code, as the UPS adapter does, and keeps
    only offers for the method's Amazon Shipping services. An Amazon Shipping service
    nobody has authored is recorded as an observation and dropped, like a mail class
    missing from the USPS allow-list. Only Amazon Shipping Ground is known, and only from
    the sandbox; production has never quoted it for us.
  - It issues its own Offers, because a purchase needs Amazon's `requestToken` and
    `rateId`. `ShippingRateService`'s offer loop already leaves those alone. Each Offer
    names the connection and carries its carrier and service ids (`02`).
  - It never quotes an Amazon-originating order, even when that order's connection is
    inactive. Sold `EXTERNAL`, its label would lose its link to the Amazon order
    (ADR-0002).
  - Special services: the sandbox `EXTERNAL` rate carried no value-added service groups.
    Until a production quote shows otherwise, the adapter reports every special service
    as not implemented, so a shipment that requires one gets no Amazon Shipping offer.
- **Buying, printing, tracking, voiding and recovery are unchanged.** The Label records
  the connection as its postage source, and dispatch reads that, as
  `amazon-shipping-external-orders/06` proved.
- **Automation.** An Amazon Shipping rate is a direct rate. A method listing Amazon
  Shipping Ground buys it unattended when it wins under due-by, like UPS Ground.
  Approvals for orders from other channels stop being read here, and `13` removes them.
  This is a behaviour change: until now such a purchase needed an approval.
- **Dates and End of Day.** Its Labels are dated by the Amazon Shipping carrier row (`08`).
  They now carry a `normalized_carrier_id`, which `amazon-shipping-external-orders/06`
  found null because no row matched. End of Day lists Amazon Shipping with no manifest.
- **Configuration.** A method that asked Amazon for other channels through the hook row
  lists Amazon Shipping Ground instead. A rule that wants it names *Direct, Amazon
  Shipping Ground* (`07`).

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] A Shopify order on a method listing Amazon Shipping Ground, with a connection
      scoped, is quoted Amazon Shipping Ground beside the other direct rates
- [ ] The same method gives an Amazon order no `EXTERNAL` quote. Its Amazon Shipping comes
      only through Buy Shipping, when the method allows Buy Shipping
- [ ] Batch ship buys Amazon Shipping Ground for that Shopify order unattended, with no
      approval
- [ ] A `403 A-101` still reports the connection as not set up and marks it so
- [ ] Buying, tracking, voiding and recovery behave as `amazon-shipping-external-orders/06`
      proves
- [ ] Off-Amazon scopes sit on Amazon Shipping, and
      `DataSource::resolveOffAmazonShipping()` resolves as before
- [ ] The Carrier Account form sends Amazon Shipping to Connections, and a
      `CarrierAccount` for it is refused
- [ ] An Amazon Shipping Label is dated by Amazon Shipping's carrier row and carries its
      `normalized_carrier_id`
