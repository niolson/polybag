# Cutting a street line to the carrier's limit can drop the house number

> *This was generated by AI during release-polish pass 4.*

Status: done — 2026-11-18

Pass: 4 — bug (review of label address characters and Locations outside the Americas)

Page or flow: Ship page purchase and batch ship, FedEx and USPS (and UPS once the previous finding cuts)

Severity: medium — the label prints a different, incomplete address with no warning; mostly international (extension), but long US lines with a unit number hit it too

Verified: plausible. Line numbers are at `12592f33`; this area is unchanged on `dfd842c5`.

## Problem

`FedexAdapter::buildStreetLines()` (`app/Services/Carriers/FedexAdapter.php`
line ~1632) keeps the first 35 characters of each street line and throws the rest away;
`UspsAdapter::buildDomesticAddress()` / `buildInternationalAddress()` (lines 2207, 2216,
2230, 2247) keep 50. In most of Europe and Latin America the house number comes after the
street name, so the tail is exactly what is lost. Transliteration makes this slightly
more likely, since `ß` → `ss` and Cyrillic `Щ` → `Shch` lengthen the line before the cut.
`AddressForm` allows 255 characters for `address1`, and imports are not limited at all.

## Evidence

Scenario: a FedEx International label to a Shipment whose `address1` is
`Avenida Doutor Francisco Sá Carneiro 1234` (40 characters) and `address2` empty. FedEx
receives `Avenida Doutor Francisco Sa Carneir` — no number — and the 2D barcode carries
the same. A US Shipment `12345 North Saint Clair Shores Boulevard Apt 1207` (49
characters) sent to FedEx loses `ulevard Apt 1207`, the unit included. Nothing tells the
packer; the Shipment and pack slip keep the full line.

## What to build

Product decision between: (a) wrap the overflow into the next free
street line (FedEx takes up to three, UPS three, USPS has `secondaryAddress`), and refuse
with a message naming the field only when it still does not fit; or (b) refuse the
purchase before anything is sent when a line exceeds the limit, so the operator
shortens it on the Shipment. Either way, never cut silently.

## Comments

- 2026-11-18 — Decided: **refuse**, option (b). Nothing PolyBag sends a carrier may differ
  from the Shipment's address without strict rules and a clear record of what was sent,
  since a misshipment has to be traceable to the exact request. Wrapping was checked first
  against a year of real orders (2025, 422,650 orders, 893 with an address line 1 over 35
  characters, all US or Guam; the data stays out of the repo). On those raw, unvalidated
  lines, wrap-then-refuse would still have refused 95 FedEx labels (2 × 35), 2 UPS (3 × 35)
  and 3 USPS (2 × 50); 68 of the FedEx 95 were delivery notes in line 1 with the street in
  line 2. Refusing every line over the limit instead is at most 893 FedEx/UPS labels
  (0.21% of orders) and 113 USPS (0.03%), before address validation, which should replace
  most of them with a standardized street first. FedEx Ship Manager's own advice is to
  shorten with common abbreviations, so the refusal names the field, the limit and that
  remedy.
- 2026-11-18 — FedEx street line 3: the Ship API accepts three lines ("streetlines more
  than 3 will be ignored"). A sandbox probe could not show whether a third line prints:
  the sandbox label overwrites line 2 with "TEST LABEL - DO NOT SHIP", and line 3 appeared
  nowhere, including the 2D barcode, which does carry lines 1 and 2. Treat FedEx as two
  lines unless a production label shows otherwise. Captures in
  `.scratch/release-polish/fedex-street-line-3/`.

- 2026-11-18 — Built as decided. FedEx, UPS and USPS implement a new
  `LimitsAddressFieldLengths` contract (FedEx 35/35, UPS 35/30, USPS 50 with a city of 28,
  or 30 for an international recipient), and `AddressFieldTooLong` names the first street
  line or city of the ship-from or ship-to address over the limit, measured as entered and
  as ASCII so every label format gets the same answer. `ShippingRateService` leaves such a
  carrier out with that reason, which the Ship page shows as an exclusion and batch ship now
  gives as its failure instead of "no rates"; the purchase checks again before the offer is
  claimed (`PackageShippingResult::addressTooLong()`), which catches a Location street
  changed after quoting, since the offer binds only the origin's postal code and city. The
  adapters no longer cut street lines or city. Names are still cut, to a fixed rule, by
  `NameLimit`, which logs the field and lengths (not the name) on the carrier's channel.
  Tests in `SourceFirstRatingTest` and the three adapter tests.
- 2026-11-18 — FedEx Ship Manager labels address line 3 "(express only)", so a third line
  is at best conditional on the service. PolyBag keeps FedEx to two lines.
