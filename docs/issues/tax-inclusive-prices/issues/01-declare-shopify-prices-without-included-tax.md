# Declare Shopify prices without included tax

Status: needs-triage
Category: bug
Repo: `polybag`

> *This was generated by AI during triage.*

## Parent

None. Opened 2026-11-02 from the review of `order-currency/03`.

## Problem

Since `order-currency/03`, the Shopify import values each item at what the buyer paid, in
the currency they paid in: the line item's `originalUnitPriceSet.presentmentMoney` less
its discount allocations. Where a market shows prices with tax included (UK or EU buyers
shown duty- and tax-inclusive prices, for example), that price may include VAT.

A customs value excludes VAT, and so do the thresholds PolyBag checks the Shipment's value
against: the UK's £135 and the EU's €150 IOSS limit. A tax-inclusive price would overstate
the declared value and could put an order over a threshold it is under.

The old import, which read `shopMoney`, had the same exposure. Declaring in the buyer's
currency makes tax-inclusive markets the common case for international Shopify orders.

## To find out

On a development store with a tax-inclusive market (a UK or EU market with taxes
included in prices):

- Place an order, with a discount, paid in that market's currency.
- Capture `order.taxesIncluded`, and on each line `originalUnitPriceSet`, its
  `discountAllocations` and `taxLines { priceSet rate title }`, each in presentment money.
- Check whether `originalUnitPriceSet` includes the tax, and whether the discount
  allocations are tax-inclusive too.
- Save the raw responses to `.scratch/tax-inclusive-prices/`. The read-only capture
  script from `order-currency/03` (`.scratch/order-currency/shopify-order-prices.php`) is
  a starting point.

Then decide whether the import subtracts the line's tax when `taxesIncluded` is true, and
check the query cost: the open fulfillment orders page is at 922 of Shopify's 1,000
points (`order-currency/03`).
