# SMS for MFA codes and alert notifications

Status: needs-triage
Category: enhancement
Created: 2026-11-19

**Parked.** This is a candidate from the list of things PolyBag could support, not a
requested feature. Nobody is waiting on it, and nothing here is scheduled.

## Problem

Packers may have a phone but no company email address. For them, the MFA options are:

- **Email codes**, which need an email address they may not have.
- **An authenticator app (TOTP)**, which means installing and enrolling an app. That is
  extra onboarding work for warehouse staff.

An SMS code to their own phone avoids both.

Separately, SMS could carry **alert notifications** to staff, not only sign-in codes.

## Current state

- The panel registers two MFA providers in `AppPanelProvider`:
  - `AppAuthentication`: TOTP with recovery codes.
  - `EmailAuthenticationForEmailUsers`: email codes, offered only to users who have an
    email address.
- `EnsureRequiredMfaIsEnabled` enforces the `require_mfa` setting on every request.
- Filament 5 ships only the App and Email providers. SMS needs a custom class
  implementing `Filament\Auth\MultiFactor\Contracts\MultiFactorAuthenticationProvider`.
  Filament's `EmailAuthentication` is the model to follow: it generates codes, expires
  them, handles resending and builds the challenge form.
- `users` has no phone number column.
- Nothing in the app sends SMS today.

## What it would take

1. **Schema.** Add to `users`:
   - an encrypted phone number, stored in E.164 format
   - a verified-at timestamp
   - an SMS-MFA enabled flag

   The phone number is new PII, so it must be covered by `pii-retention`.
2. **Provider.**
   - Enrollment sends a code to the number and turns SMS on only after the code is
     confirmed.
   - The challenge at login sends a code and checks it.
   - Resends are rate-limited per user and per IP.
3. **Delivery.** There are two shapes, and the alerts question below decides between them:
   - **A Verify API** (Twilio Verify, Vonage Verify). The vendor generates, sends and
     checks the code; the provider calls start and check. Vendors send from their own
     pre-registered senders, so there is no carrier registration, and they include fraud
     controls.
   - **Raw SMS from our own registered sender.** We generate and check the code, as
     Filament's email provider does, and send it through a Laravel notification channel.
     Laravel maintains a first-party Vonage channel. Twilio and AWS SNS have community
     channels under `laravel-notification-channels/*`. Any of these is a new dependency
     and needs approval.
4. **Credentials.** Store them encrypted in App Settings, like other operational
   credentials, never in `.env`.
5. **Admin and recovery.**
   - `MfaResetService`, the user resource and the profile page must handle the SMS
     factor.
   - Admins need a way to clear a lost phone number.
6. **Tests.** Cover enrollment, the challenge, rate limits and the admin reset.

## Alerts change the delivery choice

A Verify API sends only its own code template. It cannot send arbitrary text, so alerts
need a sender registered with US carriers. The ways to register one are:

- **Toll-free number with toll-free verification.** This is the likely fit at PolyBag's
  volumes. There is no brand or campaign fee, and approval takes days to a couple of
  weeks. You submit a sample message, the opt-in method and the expected volume.
- **A2P 10DLC.** You register a brand and a campaign, and there are one-time and monthly
  fees. Throughput is higher, but PolyBag does not need it.
- **Short code.** Hundreds of dollars a month and months of lead time. Not justified.

If alerts are built, one registered sender can send both alerts and MFA codes. 2FA is its
own campaign use case, and a mixed campaign also exists. A Verify API's remaining
advantage is then its fraud controls. A US/Canada destination allow-list and resend rate
limits cover most of that.

So the likely design is **one SMS channel**, with encrypted credentials and a sender
number, used by two things:

- the MFA provider, which generates and checks its own codes
- Laravel notifications, sent by SMS or email according to each user's preference

If alerts are never built, a Verify API is the simpler route to SMS MFA alone.

Any registered sender also needs:

- **Recorded consent.**
  - Enrolling a phone for MFA counts as consent for MFA codes.
  - Alerts need their own opt-in checkbox, and the time of consent must be stored.
- **STOP/HELP handling.** Most providers answer the keywords themselves. The app still
  has to learn about an opt-out, through a webhook or a status check, and stop sending to
  that number.
- **A decided sender identity.**
  - **Self-hosted installs:** each operator registers their own number as their own
    business.
  - **Instances we host:** it is unresolved whether PolyBag can be the registered brand
    when messages go to each tenant's own staff, or whether every tenant needs its own
    registration. Carriers have separate rules for software platforms that send on
    behalf of other businesses. Ask the chosen provider before designing around either
    answer.

## Providers

| Option | Notes |
|---|---|
| Twilio Verify | The most common choice. Includes fraud protection against SMS pumping. |
| Vonage Verify | Similar to Twilio Verify. Retries do not add to the per-verification fee. |
| AWS End User Messaging (formerly SNS SMS) | Cheap raw SMS. You register your own 10DLC or toll-free number, and there is no managed Verify product. |
| Telnyx, Plivo, Sinch, Bird | Cheaper raw SMS. Telnyx and Plivo also have Verify products. |
| Prelude | Built specifically for one-time codes, with fraud filtering. |
| Azure Communication Services | Raw SMS, and needs registration. |

## Cost

These are list prices for US numbers as of 2026-11. Confirm them before choosing a
vendor.

| Option | Approximate cost |
|---|---|
| Twilio Verify | $0.05 per successful verification plus $0.0083 per SMS, about $0.058 in total. Sends are charged whether or not they are delivered. |
| Vonage Verify | About $0.057 per successful verification, plus the per-message rate. |
| Raw SMS (Twilio) | $0.0083 per message plus a carrier fee of $0.0035 to $0.005 (T-Mobile's rose to $0.0045 in January 2026), about $0.012 to $0.013 per message. |

Raw SMS on 10DLC also has registration fees: a one-time brand fee, a campaign vetting
fee, a monthly campaign fee and a monthly number fee. These are recalled rather than
confirmed, but in total they are tens of dollars up front and single-digit to low
double-digit dollars a month.

**Example:** 20 users signing in once each working day is about 440 sign-ins a month.

- With a Verify API: about $25 a month.
- With raw SMS: about $6 a month in messages, plus the registration fees.

Either way, money is not the obstacle. Registration lead time, and fraud exposure if
resends are not rate-limited, are.

## Security notes

- NIST SP 800-63B classes SMS as a *restricted* authenticator, because SIM swaps and
  number porting can redirect codes. Keep TOTP as the recommended factor and present SMS
  as the low-friction fallback.
- Allow only US and Canadian destination numbers unless a real need for others appears.
  This is the main protection against SMS pumping, where attackers trigger sends to
  premium-rate numbers to run up the bill.
- Some packers may not want to give their employer a personal phone number. SMS must stay
  one option among several, never the only one.
