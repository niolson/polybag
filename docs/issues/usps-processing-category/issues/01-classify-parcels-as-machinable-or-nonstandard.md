# Classify each parcel as machinable or nonstandard, and buy the USPS rate that matches

Status: needs-triage
Category: enhancement
Repo: `polybag`

> *This was generated by AI during triage.*

## Where this came from

`carrier-catalog-reset/02` sells USPS Media Mail directly. It left one question open. USPS
returns Media Mail twice, as `SP` under both `MACHINABLE` and `NONSTANDARD` at one price.
Both are shown, and automation takes whichever sorts first. Nothing decides which one a
given parcel should be bought as.

The maintainer's direction: the processing category should follow the Package's box size.
It is either classified automatically, or marked by someone. More box configurations may
be needed to express it.

This reopens a decision ADR-0005 reserved. Its *Nonstandard handling* paragraph says the
axis "is not fully derivable from measurements", that "if one is ever needed it is a
third, independent axis beside form and carrier identity", and "Reserved, not designed; no
column, no enum." Whatever this issue settles amends that paragraph.

## What USPS says

From DMM 201.7, as published on 2026-09-24. The page carries a `[7-12-26]` revision mark.
The 2023-11-06 archive of Quick Service Guide 201e is older. It had three categories
(machinable, irregular, nonmachinable) and a 6 oz machinable minimum. The current guide and
the DMM have two categories, and a minimum weight only for Marketing Mail. The USPS API
speaks the current vocabulary, `MACHINABLE` and `NONSTANDARD`.

- **Two categories** (201.7.1): "machinable or nonstandard. These categories are based on
  the physical dimensions of the piece, and the placement of the delivery address and
  other required labels on the piece."
- **Machinable** (201.7.5):
  - "Not more than 22 inches long, or 18 inches wide, or 15 inches high."
  - "Not more than 25 pounds." No minimum weight, except 3.5 oz for Marketing Mail.
  - No numeric minimum size. A piece must be "large enough to hold" the address, labels,
    postage and barcode "on a single optical plane".
- **Nonstandard by dimensions or weight** (201.7.6.1): "more than 22 inches long, or 18
  inches wide, or 15 inches high", or "any parcel that weighs more than 25 pounds".
- **Nonstandard by characteristics** (201.7.6.2):
  - "Cylindrical tubes or rolls."
  - "A can, or wood or metal box."
  - "More than 24 ounces of liquid in glass containers, or 1 gallon or more of liquid in
    metal or plastic containers (see 601.3.4)."
  - "Other (e.g., parcels prepared under 601.3.0 and 601.4.0 with contents that may cause
    the parcel to roll or not maintain the integrity of the packaging during processing)."
- **Soft packs.** QSG 201e: "Soft goods wrapped in paper or plastic bags and printed matter
  are machinable only if all packaging standards in 601 are met." DMM 601 has not been
  read for this issue.
- **Length** is "the distance of the longest dimension" (201.7.3.2).

## What the app does today

- `UspsAdapter` never sends a `processingCategory` in the `search` request. It asks for
  `ALL_OUTBOUND` domestically, or `ALL` internationally.
- It keeps every category that comes back except these:
  - `CARDS`, `LETTERS` and `OPEN_AND_DISTRIBUTE`;
  - `FLATS`, except the flat-rate envelopes.

  The rate's category goes into its metadata, and `createDomesticShipment()` sends it back
  in the label request. So the category bought is the category of the rate someone chose,
  not a fact about the parcel.
- `BoxSizeType` is `BOX`, `POLYBAG` or `PADDED_MAILER`, and chooses USPS cubic tiers only.
  Nothing on `BoxSize`, `Package` or `Product` records machinability, or any of the
  characteristics above.
- A Package carries its measured length, width, height and weight. A Manual Ship Package
  has no box size.

## What the logs show

The local `storage/logs/usps-validation-*.log` files hold 216 sandbox `search` responses,
from 2026-09-06 to 09-21. The parcels ranged from 4×6×6 in to 12×12×14 in and from 0.15 lb
to 3.1 lb. All were within the machinable limits.

- **Ground Advantage, Priority Mail, Priority Mail Express:** `MACHINABLE` only, plus
  `FLATS` for the flat-rate envelopes.
- **Media Mail, Library Mail, Bound Printed Matter:** `MACHINABLE` and `NONSTANDARD` every
  time, at one price. For Media Mail that was 195 of 195 responses.
- **Marketing Mail parcels:** `NONSTANDARD` at 0.15 lb and `MACHINABLE` above it. Not sold
  here.

So for the classes sold here, USPS chooses the category for Ground Advantage and Priority,
and leaves it to the shipper for Media Mail. Nothing oversized or tube-shaped has been
quoted. What USPS returns for a nonstandard parcel, and at what price, is unknown.

## Direction

Recommended. The maintainer settles the open questions below.

1. **One classification, in one place.** A Package is nonstandard when any of these holds,
   and machinable otherwise:
   - its box size declares a characteristic the measurements cannot show (see 2);
   - its sorted dimensions exceed 22 × 18 × 15 in;
   - its weight exceeds 25 lb.

   A Manual Ship Package is classified by its measurements alone. `PackageData` carries the
   result, as it carries `qualifyingContents`, so the result is part of the offer's
   fingerprint. Re-boxing a parcel then retires an offer quoted for the old box.
2. **The box size declares what measurements cannot show.** The options:
   - **(a) A `TUBE` form**, a new `BoxSizeType` case beside `POLYBAG`. A tube is a physical
     form, so this is ADR-0005's first axis rather than a new one. It is classified
     nonstandard automatically. It says nothing about cans or wood or metal boxes.
   - **(b) A declaration on the box size**, nullable. Unset means "decide from the
     measurements", and set means "nonstandard whatever its size". An operator marks
     tubes, cans and wood or metal boxes. It works in one direction only: a box whose
     measurements exceed the limits can never be marked machinable.
   - **(c) Both.** `TUBE` for the common shape, which UPS and FedEx also treat specially,
     and the declaration for materials.

   Recommended: (b), and add (a) when another carrier's handling needs the shape. (b)
   covers every characteristic with one field, and (a) alone leaves cans and wood or metal
   boxes unexpressed.
3. **The adapter buys the category the Package is.**
   - Where USPS returns one class and indicator under both categories, as with Media Mail
     today, keep only the rate that matches the Package. Media Mail then appears once.
   - Where USPS returns one category and it disagrees with the Package, such as a
     12 × 4 × 4 in tube that USPS rates `MACHINABLE`, the probe below decides. Send the
     category in the `search` request if that changes the price. Otherwise either drop the
     rate, or buy it as the Package's category and accept the label's price.
4. **Probe first.** `search` costs nothing, and one label settles the purchase side. Record
   the findings here, as `packaging-form-and-carrier-identity/05` did:
   - `search` an oversized parcel, for example 24 × 12 × 10 in. Which categories come back
     for Ground Advantage, Priority and Media Mail, and at what prices?
   - `search` a small parcel with `processingCategory: NONSTANDARD`. `05` found that
     `MACHINABLE`, `FLATS` and no category gave byte-identical results for a small box, but
     it did not try `NONSTANDARD`.
   - Buy a label as `NONSTANDARD` for a machinable-size parcel. Is it accepted, refused or
     re-priced? Then buy one as `MACHINABLE` for an oversized parcel.

## Open questions

1. Which box-size declaration: (a), (b) or (c)?
2. USPS-specific or carrier-neutral? A USPS column is the narrow answer. ADR-0005's
   reserved axis is carrier-neutral. UPS and FedEx also surcharge cylinders and parcels not
   fully encased in corrugated, which `ups-rate-accuracy` touches.
3. Contents that make a parcel nonstandard (liquids over the thresholds, contents that
   roll) belong to the product, like `hazmat_class`. Should this issue add them, or record
   them as later work?
4. Are soft packs machinable by default? Read DMM 601 before answering.
5. What happens when USPS and the Package disagree? The probe decides.

Shopify and Amazon purchases are out of scope. PolyBag does not send a processing category
for either.
