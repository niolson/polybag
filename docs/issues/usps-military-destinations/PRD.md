# USPS military and diplomatic destinations

Status: reference

Repo: `polybag`

Background for the issues in this directory. Not a work item.

> *This was generated by AI during triage.*

## Where this came from

`packaging-form-and-carrier-identity/05` taught direct USPS to keep the flat-rate
indicators, and in doing so had to decide what to do with `PM` — USPS's rate indicator
for the Large Flat Rate Box at its APO/FPO/DPO price. It is dropped there, because the
adapter has no destination rule and offering it to every destination would have made it
win every large-box comparison. That drop is the first item below; looking for the rest
of what a military destination changes found the others.

Everything here was read off the USPS sandbox `search` endpoint on 2026-09-16 with
three overseas military ZIPs (one each in AA, AE and AP), a warehouse origin, and a
small-box package. No labels were bought. Findings that depend on the label endpoint
are marked unverified.

## What the app already does for a military address

- `AddressData::isMilitary()` recognises `AA`/`AE`/`AP` as the state and `APO`/`FPO`/`DPO`
  as the city.
- `ShippingRateService` only asks a carrier for rates when it has a cataloged service
  marked `can_ship_to_military_addresses`; every seeded USPS service is, UPS and FedEx
  are not (except the USPS-last-mile products), so a military shipment rates through
  USPS and Amazon Buy Shipping.
- `UspsAdapter::createShipment()` attaches a customs form to a domestic label whenever
  the destination is military, because USPS rejects one without it.

## What USPS returns for a military destination that the app does not account for

| Observation | Sandbox evidence | App today |
|---|---|---|
| `PM` — Large Flat Rate Box at the APO/FPO/DPO price — is returned alongside `PL`, $1.41 cheaper, for **every** destination, military or not | AA, AE, AP and two domestic ZIPs all return both | `PM` dropped everywhere; a military large box pays the `PL` price |
| Priority Mail and Ground Advantage carry `commitment.name: "MILITARY"` with **no** `scheduleDeliveryDate` and `guaranteedDelivery: false` | all three ZIPs | `RateSelector::isOnTime()` treats a dateless rate as *not* on time, so a shipping rule with a delivery deadline can never pick them — see `02` |
| Priority Mail Express is offered to all three ZIPs; to AE and AP it says `"2 Days"`, `guaranteedDelivery: true`, with a scheduled date; to AA it says `"MILITARY"` | AE: 2 days at $63.94, AP: 2 days at $52.06, AA: `MILITARY` at $63.94 | Shown as quoted. With a deadline rule it is the only USPS rate with a date, so it is what automation buys. Whether USPS honours a guarantee to an APO — its public description of Priority Mail Express Military Service says it does not — is unverified |
| Military zones are 07/08 for Priority Mail regardless of continent (the zone is to the gateway) | AE 07, AP 07, AA 08 | Nothing depends on zone; noted only so a "wrong zone" report is not chased |
| Flat-rate envelopes and boxes are all offered to military ZIPs | every indicator `05` classifies came back | Handled by `05`; nothing military-specific |

## Not observed, worth knowing before buying

- Whether the label endpoint accepts `PM` for a non-military address (it should not) and
  `PM`/Priority Mail Express for a military one. One sandbox buy each answers it.
- USPS publishes per-ZIP restrictions for overseas military mail (size, weight, and
  content prohibitions that vary by post office). The rate endpoint does not apply them;
  whether the label endpoint does is unknown. The app has no representation of them.
- Address validation of `PSC`/`Unit`/`CMR` box formats through the Google and USPS
  validators has not been looked at.
- Tracking for military mail ends at the gateway or the military post office; the
  existing "delivered to agent" handling may or may not cover the scan USPS emits.
